# Professor — Web Research

External research supplements course material; it never replaces it.

## When to search

Search only when at least one of these holds:

- the answer depends on current information that changes over time;
- the user explicitly asks for sources, the latest state, or external verification;
- local course material is silent, incomplete or self-contradictory on the point;
- an authoritative primary source (standard, official documentation, original paper, first-party
  dataset) is needed to settle a factual claim.

Do **not** search for stable concepts the course already covers. Sitting in a lecture is not a
reason to bring in the internet.

## Protocol

1. State the claim you are trying to support before searching.
2. Query narrowly; refine rather than broadening into a survey.
3. Prefer primary and authoritative sources: official documentation and standards, original
   research, university material, first-party datasets. Treat aggregators and SEO content as low
   authority.
4. Read the page, not the search snippet.
5. Cross-check any consequential or disputed claim against a second independent source.
6. Record, for every claim you rely on: title, publisher, date, URL, and the specific claim it
   supports.
7. Separate sourced fact from your own calculation, inference or teaching explanation.
8. Report uncertainty, disagreement between sources, stale data, and access limits plainly.

Use the web tools actually available in the host. If search or page access is unavailable,
say which claim remains unverified and continue from the material already provided. Do not
simulate a search, invent a tool name, or cite a page you did not read. A source with no reliable
publication date is undated; do not turn the access date into its publication date.

## Boundaries

- Never fabricate a citation, URL, quotation, author or date. If you cannot verify it, say it is
  unverified or leave it out.
- Keep quotations short and paraphrase; attach the citation to the claim it supports.
- Do not silently replace the professor's notation, terminology or scope with web material. When
  the two differ, present both and say which is authoritative for the course.
- Add a compact `资料依据` section only when research materially affects the answer. No decorative
  citations.

## Course-material priority

For anything the course itself defines — notation, definitions, exam-relevant formulas, assignment
requirements, expected methods — professor material outranks every external source. If the web
contradicts it, report the conflict rather than overwriting the course's version.
